Literary Heritage

Tulsi Manas Temple

Durgakund Road, opposite Durga Kund, southern Varanasi

Tulsi Manas Temple: the radiant white marble temple commemorating the site where Tulsidas composed the Ramcharitmanas in 1574 CE.

Standing in luminous white marble amidst landscaped lawns directly opposite the historic Durga Kund, the Tulsi Manas Temple is both a place of worship and an architectural homage to the most influential literary work in North Indian history. Built upon the exact spot where the poet-saint Goswami Tulsidas lived and composed the Ramcharitmanas in 1574 CE, the sanctuary literally transcribes the entire Awadhi epic onto its interior marble walls.

The Vernacular Revolution: Awakening the Epics

Until the sixteenth century, the narrative of Lord Rama was preserved predominantly in classical Sanskrit through the Valmiki Ramayana—accessible primarily to elite scholarly brahmins. In 1574 CE, defying conservative religious opposition in Varanasi, Goswami Tulsidas began composing the Ramcharitmanas in Awadhi, the spoken dialect of everyday people.

This vernacular epic democratized spiritual literature across northern India, allowing common laborers, farmers, and women to chant and comprehend the story of Rama. The title itself translates as the "Sacred Lake of the Deeds of Rama" (Manas). It is believed that Tulsidas resided under a hut in this exact mango orchard while writing the seven books (kandas) of the poem, concluding his work under the inspiration of divine encounters.

Key Historical Facts

  • Historic Spot: Residence of Goswami Tulsidas during the composition of the Ramcharitmanas (1574 CE)
  • Current Temple Built: 1964 CE by the philanthropic Sureka industrialist family of Howrah, West Bengal
  • Inaugurated By: Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, second President of India
  • Architectural Material: Pure white Makrana marble from Rajasthan
  • Unique Feature: All 10,000+ verses (chaupais and dohas) of the Ramcharitmanas are etched into the interior marble walls

A Living Book of Marble and Sculpture

Commissioned in the early 1960s by the industrialist Thakur Das Sureka and inaugurated in 1964 by India’s philosopher-president Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, the temple is designed as a physical manifestation of the epic text. As visitors enter the grand two-storey hall, they are surrounded by hundreds of polished marble panels upon which every single couplet (doha) and quatrain (chaupai) of the Ramcharitmanas has been meticulously carved and filled with gold and black leaf.

Above the engraved verses, colorful bas-relief marble friezes and intricate murals depict key scenes: Rama’s departure into exile, Sita’s abduction, the building of the bridge to Lanka, and the triumphant return to Ayodhya. On the second floor, a beloved historical attraction is the gallery of mechanical dioramas created by Rajasthani artisans, where motorized animated figures re-enact scenes from the Ramayana, accompanied by soft instrumental music.

Sanctuary & Modern Pilgrimage Significance

The primary sanctum houses exquisite marble idols of Lord Rama, Sita, Lakshmana, and Hanuman. Flanking the main hall is an adjacent shrine dedicated to Goswami Tulsidas himself, depicted sitting with a quill and palm-leaf manuscript in hand. Devotees often sit quietly on the cool marble floors with handheld prayer beads or miniature editions of the scripture, singing chaupais aloud.

Surrounded by well-kept ornamental gardens planted with flowering shrubs and tulsi (holy basil) groves, Tulsi Manas Temple provides a tranquil, contemplative environment. It offers travelers an opportunity to appreciate the profound linguistic and poetic heritage that defines Varanasi alongside its stone temples and ritual waters.

Visitor Information & Guidelines

Timings
Open daily from 05:30 to 12:00, and 15:30 to 21:00. The mechanical puppet gallery on the upper floor operates between 16:00 and 20:00.
Admission
Entry to the main temple hall is free; a nominal 10-rupee ticket is collected for the upper-floor animated diorama museum.
Photography
Photography is allowed in the gardens and main courtyard. Discreet mobile photography of the engraved marble wall verses is permitted.
Location
Situated on Sankat Mochan Road / Durgakund crossing, directly opposite Durga Kund. 5 km south of Varanasi Cantt station.

Recommended Visit: Plan a combined afternoon walking tour visiting Durga Kund Temple first, crossing over to Tulsi Manas, and concluding with evening tea near Sankat Mochan.
