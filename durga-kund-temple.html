Sacred Temples

Durga Kund Temple

Durgakund Road, southern sector of Varanasi

Durga Kund Temple: the striking ochre-red shrine built by Bengali queen Rani Bhabani in the eighteenth century.

With its soaring multistorey shikhara painted in deep vermilion and its peaceful adjacent rectangular water reservoir, Durga Kund Temple—often referred to colloquially by colonial visitors as the "Monkey Temple"—is one of Varanasi’s most visually iconic eighteenth-century sanctuaries. Dedicated to the goddess Durga in her fierce, protective form, the temple stands as an enduring monument of royal Bengali philanthropy in Kashi.

Royal Patronage & The Queen of Natore

While an ancient shrine commemorating the goddess's victory over the demon Durgamasur had stood on this territory since Puranic antiquity, the present architectural masterpiece was commissioned in the eighteenth century by Rani Bhabani (1702–1802), the formidable queen of the Natore estate in northern Bengal. Known throughout eighteenth-century India as an enlightened administrator and prolific patron of Hindu heritage, Rani Bhabani endowed hundreds of temples across Bengal and Kashi.

In designing Durga Kund, she fused regional North Indian Nagara temple architecture with the distinctive red-stained stucco tradition of Bengal's brick temples. The main spire is composed of intricately stepped tiers that taper gracefully toward a gilded kalasha finial, while the entire outer facade is painted in intense red ochre—the color sacred to the Mother Goddess symbolizing creative feminine energy (shakti) and cosmic vitality.

Key Historical Facts

  • Presiding Deity: Goddess Durga (Swayambhu / self-manifested stone icon)
  • Commissioned By: Rani Bhabani of Natore (Bengal) during the 1760s
  • Architectural Style: Nagara style with stepped shikhara towers and Bengal-influenced terracotta aesthetics
  • Adjoining Tank: Durga Kund, a large rectangular stone-stepped reservoir historically fed by the Ganges
  • Peak Celebrations: Navratri festivals (Chaitra and Sharad) and the Nag Panchami fair

The Sacred Kund and The Swayambhu Murti

Inside the square sanctum, the idol of Durga is worshipped as a Swayambhu (self-manifested) stone icon that was not carved by human hand. The idol is adorned with elaborate silk robes, gold ornaments, and garlands of fragrant red hibiscus flowers. Devotees offer coconuts, red chunris (veils), and sindoor, seeking the goddess's divine protection from calamity, sickness, and spiritual ignorance.

Adjacent to the temple complex lies the Durga Kund itself, a vast rectangular water body lined with stone steps and retaining walls. In historical times, the reservoir was connected to the Ganges by subterranean canals that ensured a continuous inflow of freshwater. Although the canal links are now closed, the kund retains stone pavilions (chhatris) at its corners where pilgrims gather during festivals to perform purification rites.

Navratri Celebrations & Sacred Geography

During the biannual festival of Navratri (the Nine Nights of the Goddess), Durga Kund becomes a major focal point for pilgrimage in Uttar Pradesh. Devotees stand in queues extending deep into surrounding streets from before sunrise to offer their respects. The fourth day of Navratri, dedicated to Goddess Kushmanda, is celebrated with special solemnity here, as ancient texts equate the resident deity of this temple with Kushmanda, who created the universe with her cosmic laughter.

Durga Kund also occupies a central role in the traditional Durga Yatra, a prescribed circuit of temples honoring nine forms of the divine mother scattered across Varanasi. Its location on Durgakund Road places it immediately within walking distance of the Tulsi Manas Temple, creating a harmonious walking itinerary for travelers exploring the religious geography of southern Varanasi.

Visitor Information & Guidelines

Timings
Open daily from 05:00 to 23:00. The temple remains open continuously during Navratri festival periods.
Photography
Exterior photography of the temple spires and the historic kund is permitted; photography inside the inner sanctum is strictly prohibited.
Offerings
Traditional offerings of red hibiscus, dry coconuts, and sindoor can be purchased from licensed vendors lining the temple lane.
Accessibility
Positioned directly on main Durgakund Road; fully reachable by auto-rickshaw or taxi without pedestrian-only limitations.

Traveler's Tip: Visit during early morning or sunset hours when the amber light illuminates the red towers across the waters of the kund, providing one of Varanasi's most photogenic architectural views.
