Bharat Mata Temple

Mahatma Gandhi Kashi Vidyapith campus, central Varanasi

Bharat Mata Temple: a rare non-sectarian temple honoring the motherland, inaugurated by Mahatma Gandhi in 1936.

In a city celebrated for its thousands of deities, the Bharat Mata Temple stands as a singular, radical architectural departure. Situated within the campus of the Mahatma Gandhi Kashi Vidyapith, this serene sanctuary contains no anthropomorphic statues of gods or goddesses. Instead, the central object of reverence is a colossal topographical three-dimensional relief map of undivided India, masterfully chiseled from pure white Makrana marble.

Conception & The Vision of Babu Shiv Prasad Gupta

The temple was envisioned and funded by Babu Shiv Prasad Gupta, a visionary nationalist patron and philanthropist, alongside the eminent Hindi scholar Dr. Bhagwan Das. Constructed between 1918 and 1924 during the high tide of India’s independence movement, the monument was designed to transcend communal divisions by offering a shared, sacred sanctuary open to Indians of all faiths, castes, and creeds.

On October 25, 1936, the temple was formally inaugurated by Mahatma Gandhi. In his dedication address, Gandhi famously praised the temple as an unprecedented civic expression of national unity, noting that "this temple contains no images of gods or goddesses. It contains only a map of Mother India... I hope this temple will serve as a cosmopolitan platform for people of all religions, provinces, and backgrounds to realize their brotherhood."

Key Historical Facts

  • Inaugurated: October 25, 1936, by Mahatma Gandhi and Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan
  • Patrons & Creators: Babu Shiv Prasad Gupta and philosopher Dr. Bhagwan Das
  • Geographical Scale: Accurate relief model scaled horizontally at 1 inch to 3.125 miles, and vertically at 1 inch to 2,000 feet
  • Material: 762 individual slabs of Makrana marble, each precisely carved to depict mountain contours
  • Territorial Scope: Depicts the undivided subcontinent, spanning from the Himalayas to Sri Lanka, Afghanistan, and Myanmar

Cartographic Mastery in Makrana Marble

The centerpiece of the temple is an extraordinary cartographic feat created by thirty master stonemasons under the technical direction of Durga Prasad Khatri. Installed flush within a slightly sunken central pit surrounded by a polished brass balustrade, the relief map measures approximately 31 feet by 30 feet.

Every major geographical feature of the subcontinent is rendered to painstaking mathematical scale. Over four hundred mountain peaks—including Everest, K2, Kanchenjunga, and Nanda Devi—are precisely elevated, while the plateaus, coastal ranges, and low-lying river valleys of the Indus, Ganges, and Brahmaputra are carved with delicate topographical precision. On Independence Day (August 15) and Republic Day (January 26), the oceans surrounding the marble landmass are symbolically filled with water and decorated with fresh flowers.

A Monument of the Indian Renaissance

Architecturally, the building reflects the restraint and dignity of early-twentieth-century Indian institutional design. Supported by five pillared arches representing the five primordial elements (panchabhuta)—earth, water, fire, air, and ether—the structure demonstrates how Varanasi participated actively in the intellectual and cultural renaissance of the freedom struggle.

Visiting Bharat Mata Temple provides travelers with a unique intellectual counterbalance to the ancient liturgical world of the riverfront ghats. It illustrates how the concept of sacred geography, deeply ingrained in Hindu pilgrimage traditions, was reimagined in the modern era to articulate a pluralistic, unifying vision of nationhood.

Visitor Information & Guidelines

Timings
Open daily from 07:00 to 17:30. Open year-round with no religious restriction on entry.
Admission
Free admission; visitors remove footwear at the entrance portico.
Viewing Balconies
Elevated walkways allow visitors to look down onto the marble relief map from multiple angles to appreciate the topographical contours.
Location
Situated inside the Kashi Vidyapith campus on Vidyapeeth Road, roughly 1.5 km south of the Varanasi Junction railway station (Varanasi Cantt).

Educational Value: An exceptional destination for students, geography enthusiasts, and history scholars interested in the intersection of Indian nationalism and traditional craft.
